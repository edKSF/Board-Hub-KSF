Wilbur Repp CTE Scholarship Program

Building a Pipeline from Kent HS to Career & Community Leadership
8-Month Development Plan · Kent Schools Foundation · Honoring the Estate Gift Letter of Intent of September 15, 2020
Visual framework: the seven-stage pipeline guiding program design (Source: KSF Board Presentation, Slide 6).

1. Honoring the Donor's Intent

On September 15, 2020, the Kent Schools Foundation accepted a Letter of Intent from Mr. Wilbur Repp directing a future estate gift to assist "struggling students who wish to pursue education and training toward a vocational trade or workforce career." This program is built around that promise. Every design choice that follows traces back to a clause in the Letter of Intent.

Allocation of the Estate Gift

75% — Wilbur Repp Endowment for Vocational Training

  • Post-secondary scholarships for KSD students pursuing vocational education and training
  • Managed within KSF's 501(c)(3) structure
  • Up to $40,000/year of endowment principal accessible
  • Designed to sustain a minimum 25-year scholarship program
  • Annual earnings fund the awards

25% — Restricted Fund

  • Expand hands-on training in high school for immediate workforce entry
  • Promote career pathways into the trades
  • Create extracurricular experiences with industry and community partners
  • Funds in-school programming, equipment, employer engagement, signature events

Eligibility Anchors from the Letter of Intent

  • Applicants demonstrate an interest in vocational training or apprenticeship programs after high school.
  • Applicants are on a path to graduate high school and have struggled academically.
  • Applicants are recommended by educators knowledgeable of their career path.
  • Final selections are made by the KSF Scholarship Committee or Board.

2. The Program Pipeline (Slide 6 Framework)

Seven distinct stages move a Kent School District student from high school graduation through to community leadership. Each stage is a service offering with owners, partners, and measurable outcomes.

1. Kent HS Graduate

Entry
What happens: Student graduates from a Kent School District high school CTE-ready: prerequisites met, FAFSA/WASFA filed, post-secondary plan in hand.
Owners / partners: KSD CTE Director, school counselors, KSD Career Specialists
Success signal: Cohort identified, applications submitted, eligibility verified.

2. Wilbur Repp CTE Scholarship

Award & Cohort
What happens: Selected scholars receive a multi-year, renewable scholarship and join an annual cohort with leadership development and academic coaching.
Owners / partners: KSF Scholarship Committee, KSF Program Manager, Cohort Coach
Success signal: Award letters, signed Scholar Agreements, cohort onboarding complete.

3. Post-Secondary Pathway

Education
What happens: Scholar enrolls in an eligible technical college, registered apprenticeship, or industry-recognized certification program.
Owners / partners: Green River College, Renton Technical College, Bates, registered WA apprenticeships
Success signal: Enrollment confirmed; quarterly term plan filed.

4. Industry Mentor

Guidance
What happens: Each scholar is matched 1:1 with a vetted industry mentor providing career guidance, resume/portfolio review, and workplace navigation.
Owners / partners: Local trade associations, alumni, employer partners
Success signal: Mentor matched within 30 days of enrollment; minimum monthly check-ins.

5. Internship / Apprenticeship

Paid Experience
What happens: Scholar completes at least one paid internship, on-the-job training rotation, or apprenticeship hour-bank milestone.
Owners / partners: Employer partners, JATCs, KSD CTE work-based learning office
Success signal: ≥ 200 paid hours; supervisor evaluation on file.

6. Early Career

Launch
What happens: Scholar transitions to a stable, family-wage role aligned with their training. KSF tracks 1-year and 3-year employment outcomes.
Owners / partners: Employer partners, Workforce Snohomish/SKCWDC where applicable
Success signal: Job placement within 6 months of completion; ≥ 80% retention at 12 months.

7. Return to Community

Pay it Forward
What happens: Alumni return as mentors, employer-host partners, classroom speakers, and donor advocates — closing the loop and seeding the next cohort.
Owners / partners: Repp Alumni Network (KSF-administered)
Success signal: ≥ 30% of alumni active annually as mentors, hosts, or speakers.

3. Scholar Eligibility & Selection

Eligibility is intentionally narrow to honor the donor's stated focus on struggling students pursuing vocational and workforce careers. It is calibrated against the Washington State Opportunity Scholarship Career & Technical Scholarship (WSOS-CTS), which most Repp Scholars will stack with this award.

Required (donor-mandated)

  • Current Kent School District student on a path to graduate high school.
  • Demonstrated interest in a vocational trade, apprenticeship, or workforce career — not a four-year bachelor's pathway.
  • Has struggled academically (e.g., GPA ≤ 2.75, credit recovery, or documented academic challenge) but is on track to graduate.
  • Recommendation from a KSD educator, CTE instructor, counselor, or workforce program lead.

Required (program standards, aligned with WSOS-CTS)

  • Washington State resident.
  • Family income at or below 125% of WA state median family income.
  • Completed FAFSA or WASFA for the upcoming aid year.
  • Post-secondary plan targeting an eligible program: high-demand trades, STEM technician, or healthcare — at a WA community/technical college, registered apprenticeship, or approved certification provider.

6. 8-Month Development Plan

The first eight months stand up the program end-to-end: charter, partnerships, applications, selection, and launch of Cohort 1. Each month has a focus, deliverables, owner, and a gateway decision the Board needs to make to move forward.

Month 1 — Charter & Mandate

Adopt the program charter; the Board signs off on the operating frame so everything else has a foundation.

Deliverables

  • Board resolution naming the Wilbur Repp CTE Scholarship Program with the 75/25 allocation locked.
  • Adopt UPMIFA-aligned spending policy (4–5% draw on 12-quarter rolling avg; up to $40K/yr principal access permitted).
  • Approve eligibility anchors and the 'no bachelor's pathway' rule.
  • Stand up the Scholarship Committee charter; seat the founding members.

Owner: Board Chair / KSF Executive Director

Gateway to Month 2: Charter & spending policy approved by Board.

Month 2 — Operating Layer

Hire the people and stand up the systems that will run the program day-to-day.

Deliverables

  • Hire or contract the Program Manager (0.5–1.0 FTE).
  • Stand up financial systems: separate fund codes for Endowment vs. Restricted Fund.
  • Adopt application platform (e.g., AwardSpring / Submittable / Foundant).
  • Draft the Scholar Agreement and Mentor Agreement (modeled on Act Six tri-party structure).

Owner: KSF Executive Director

Gateway to Month 3: Program Manager onboarded; application platform live in test mode.

Month 3 — Partnerships

Lock the partners who turn this from a scholarship into a pipeline.

Deliverables

  • Sign MOUs with Renton Technical College, Green River College, Bates Technical College, and at least one apprenticeship JATC.
  • Convene founding Industry Partner Council (target 8–12 employers).
  • Formalize the KSD partnership: CTE Director designated as standing point of contact.
  • Initial WSOS-CTS coordination call to confirm stacking and scholar referral pathways.

Owner: Program Manager

Gateway to Month 4: ≥ 4 college/JATC MOUs signed; Council seated.

Month 4 — Open Applications

Launch in every Kent comprehensive and option high school. Targeted outreach to credit-recovery and CTE pathway students — exactly the donor's audience.

Deliverables

  • Application opens; available in English and Spanish at minimum.
  • Counselor and CTE-instructor briefings at every KSD high school.
  • Educator-recommendation drive (Repp explicitly requires educator endorsements).
  • First Restricted Fund classroom-grant cycle opens in parallel for in-school CTE programming.

Owner: Program Manager + KSD CTE Director

Gateway to Month 5: ≥ 40 completed applications; ≥ 25 educator recommendations submitted.

Month 5 — Review & Interview

Two-rater rubric review, then finalist interviews. Discipline against the donor-intent rubric is the work this month.

Deliverables

  • Two-rater scoring on (a) fit with donor intent, (b) clarity of pathway, (c) demonstrated grit, (d) educator endorsement strength.
  • Calibration session for raters before scoring begins (anti-bias and donor-intent grounding).
  • Finalist interviews: panel includes a board member, an industry partner, and a KSD educator.
  • Reference checks with recommending educators.

Owner: Scholarship Committee Chair

Gateway to Month 6: Final ranked list ready for Board review.

Month 6 — Selection & Award

The Scholarship Committee or Board makes final selections — the donor explicitly named these bodies as the decision-makers.

Deliverables

  • Board approves Cohort 1 awards.
  • Award letters and Scholar Agreements signed; financial aid offices notified.
  • Mentor recruitment kicks off in parallel — vet against Industry Partner Council pool.
  • Public announcement plan with stewardship report drafted for Repp Legacy.

Owner: Board Chair + Scholarship Committee

Gateway to Month 7: ≥ 6 awards finalized; ≥ 10 vetted mentors in pipeline.

Month 7 — Onboard the Cohort

Senior Send-Off and the Summer Bridge Workshop — the moment the program becomes real for scholars.

Deliverables

  • Senior Send-Off event: award celebration, Scholar Agreement signing, cohort introductions.
  • Summer Bridge Workshop (2 days): financial literacy, tools-of-the-trade orientation, college/apprenticeship navigation.
  • Mentor Match: 1:1 pairings completed within 30 days of enrollment.
  • Tools and gear concierge stood up for cohort 1 needs.

Owner: Program Manager + Cohort Coach

Gateway to Month 8: 100% mentor match; 100% Scholar Agreement signing; Summer Bridge attended by full cohort.

Month 8 — Launch & Learn

Scholars begin their first quarter. KSF instruments the feedback loops that drive year 2 and beyond.

Deliverables

  • Quarterly cohort night (October pattern established).
  • First outcomes pulse to the Board: persistence by week 6, mid-quarter grades, mentor engagement.
  • Donor stewardship report delivered for the Repp Legacy.
  • After-Action Review on the 8-month build to inform Cohort 2 cycle.

Owner: Program Manager

Gateway to Month 9: Cohort persistence ≥ 90% at week 6; AAR memo to Board.

9. Closing — How This Honors Mr. Repp

  • The 75/25 split is preserved exactly as written in the Letter of Intent.
  • Eligibility prioritizes students "on a path to graduate but have struggled academically" — not top-of-class students.
  • Educator recommendation is required, mirroring his exact language.
  • Final awards are made by the KSF Scholarship Committee or Board.
  • Bachelor's pathways are explicitly excluded.
  • The Endowment runs to a minimum 25-year horizon, with up to $40,000/year of principal access if needed.
  • The Restricted Fund is dedicated to high school hands-on training and trades pathways.
The Slide 6 pipeline is more than a graphic — it is the operating model. When in doubt about a program decision, the test is simple: does it move a struggling Kent student through the pipeline toward a stable, family-wage trade career, and leave them positioned to come back and lift the next scholar? If yes, it is in scope. If no, it is not.
